# headline-writing

## Purpose
Create concise headline options that express one strategic idea clearly and memorably, then select based on message fit rather than cleverness alone.

## When to use
- Hero headlines, campaign lines, feature/category headings, launch messaging.
- When copy needs real conceptual alternatives rather than synonym variations.

## Inputs required
- Audience and context.
- Core problem/promise.
- Verified differentiator/proof.
- Desired action and tone constraints.
- Words/claims to avoid.

## Operating principles
- Explore conceptual territories, not just wording variants.
- Prefer concrete, ownable language over inflated benefit language.
- A headline should create useful tension or clarity without requiring the subhead to explain what it meant.
- Do not force wordplay, rhyme, alliteration, or category jargon.
- Avoid unsupported superlatives and vague transformation claims.

## Workflow
1. Restate the one idea the headline must communicate.
2. Generate 3–5 territories such as direct promise, reframing, contrast, product behavior, or audience truth.
3. Produce 2–3 lines per territory.
4. Reject lines that competitors could use unchanged.
5. Test comprehension without supporting copy.
6. Check truthfulness, rhythm, length, and visual fit.
7. Select one primary and at most two meaningful alternatives with rationale.

## Deliverables
- Territory labels.
- Headline candidates.
- Rejected-pattern notes.
- Primary recommendation and rationale.
- Claim/evidence flags where needed.

## Review criteria
- Immediate comprehension.
- Strategic specificity.
- Memorability without gimmick.
- Truthfulness.
- Visual/length fit in the intended placement.
- Low category-generic risk.

## Failure modes
- Ten synonyms for the same sentence.
- “The future of…” as a default opening.
- Clever line that hides the actual offer.
- Unsupported “best/fastest/smartest” claim.
- Headline and subhead saying the same thing twice.
- Copy that only works because of a mockup's typography.

## Handoffs
- `copywriter` owns the full message system.
- `copy-review` independently tests clarity, claims, and voice.
- `hero-section-design` uses the chosen line within the actual composition.
