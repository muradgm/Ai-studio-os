# creative-thesis

## Purpose
Author and select the central creative thesis that explains **why this project should become this kind of experience** before layout, style frames, motion, 3D, or implementation technology are chosen.

A thesis is not “premium,” “editorial,” “immersive,” “cinematic,” “minimal,” or “use WebGL.” Those are qualities or tools. The thesis must connect business/product truth to one memorable creative idea capable of governing multiple media.

## When to use
- brand-defining websites, campaigns, identities, launches, product stories, and immersive experiences;
- when research is complete enough to move from inspiration into authorship;
- when polished execution risks becoming generic because the governing idea is weak or absent;
- before `art-direction`, style-frame exploration, motion language, or creative-engineering planning.

Do not invoke when an approved/frozen thesis already exists unless new truth invalidates it.

## Inputs required
- project intent/problem;
- business/product truths with provenance;
- audience and business objective when known;
- inspiration packet with TAKE / REJECT / TRANSFORM evidence when available;
- opportunity gaps;
- category expectations and anti-references;
- useful creative traits/tensions;
- unresolved unknowns that must remain unresolved.

## Authoring workflow
1. **Restate the project truth.** What must remain true even if every visual reference disappears?
2. **Name the category default.** What would a competent but generic competitor probably make?
3. **Name the opportunity.** What documented gap could become an experience rule rather than decoration?
4. **Form 3 thesis candidates.** They must differ at the idea level, not by palette, typeface, motion intensity, or technology.
5. **Run competitor transfer.** Reject a candidate if a direct competitor could reuse it unchanged with equal credibility.
6. **Run technology deletion.** Remove WebGL, GSAP, Rive, 3D, shaders, video, AI imagery, and all implementation nouns. If the idea disappears, it was not a thesis.
7. **Run cross-modal translation.** Confirm the idea can influence typography, imagery, motion, interaction, and responsive behavior without forcing the exact same motif into every medium.
8. **Kill at least two candidates.** Record why they are weaker: generic, category-bound, spectacle-dependent, unsupported by truth, or too narrow.
9. **Select one governing idea.** State the creative tension, why it belongs to this project, and what would invalidate it.
10. **Hand off without styling it to death.** Creative World / Art Direction owns the visual-world divergence next.

## Candidate requirements
Each candidate must contain:
- governing idea in one or two sentences;
- source truth(s) it depends on;
- source opportunity gap;
- central tension;
- what category convention it rejects;
- why it belongs specifically to this project;
- one sentence on how it could influence behavior, not just appearance;
- kill risk.

## Final deliverable
Return one selected candidate with:
- `governingIdea`;
- `creativeTension`;
- `whyThisProject`;
- `principles`;
- `antiPrinciples`;
- `expressionTests` for typography, image, motion, interaction, sound, responsive;
- `technologyPolicy` stating tools serve the idea;
- `alternativesConsidered` with rejection rationale;
- `selectionRationale`;
- `competitorTransferTest`;
- unresolved risks.

The runtime will attach canonical truth/opportunity references. Do not fabricate those references inside the authored candidate.

## Quality bar
A strong thesis should make downstream specialists think, “I know what this world believes and how to make choices inside it,” while still leaving room for several genuinely different visual worlds.

Examples of weak theses:
- “A premium editorial experience with elegant motion.”
- “Use bold typography and 3D pastry renders.”
- “A cinematic WebGL journey through the brand.”
- “Minimal, warm, modern French luxury.”

Examples of stronger thesis structure:
- **Truth → tension → governing behavior.** The project-specific truth creates a tension; the experience turns that tension into a repeatable behavior or narrative rule.
- **Product mechanism → interface metaphor.** A real product/business mechanism becomes an organizing experience logic without literally illustrating it everywhere.
- **Cultural contradiction → authored point of view.** A contradiction specific to the brand/context becomes the source of hierarchy, pacing, and restraint.

## Failure modes
- starting from Awwwards effects instead of project truth;
- reskinning one idea three times and calling it exploration;
- using category clichés as “recognition” without transforming them;
- equating expensive production with distinctiveness;
- inventing audience insight, product truth, proof, or rights;
- selecting the safest candidate because it is easiest to build;
- writing a thesis so broad that every competitor could claim it.

## Handoffs
- `brand-strategist` owns upstream positioning and business meaning;
- `creative-skeptic` challenges transferability and trend dependence on high-risk work;
- `art-direction` inherits the selected thesis and generates distinct visual worlds without changing the governing idea silently;
- `creative-engineering` selects technology only after the thesis and direction justify it.
