## 3. Current reference system

### Direct category

#### TradingView
Source: https://www.tradingview.com/

**Take**
- Market context appears immediately instead of hiding the product behind abstract lifestyle imagery.
- Product breadth is demonstrated through real market surfaces.

**Reject**
- Do not inherit the information-mall feeling of a huge market portal.
- Do not compete on breadth unless TraderFrame's real product scope supports it.

#### Bloomberg Terminal
Source: https://professional.bloomberg.com/products/bloomberg-terminal/

**Take**
- Seriousness, consequence, and the knowledge-to-action narrative.
- Product communication that treats market information as decision infrastructure rather than decoration.

**Reject**
- Enterprise legacy cues and claims TraderFrame cannot substantiate.
- Complexity as a status symbol.

#### Robinhood Legend
Source: https://robinhood.com/us/en/legend/

**Take**
- Strong movement between expressive market imagery and concrete interface demonstration.
- Product states are shown rather than described only through generic feature cards.

**Reject**
- Do not reuse its “power + precision” language.
- Do not imitate metallic candlestick imagery; that territory is already occupied.

#### Koyfin
Source: https://www.koyfin.com/

**Take**
- Modern financial software can be clear and product-forward without pretending to be an investment bank.
- Real interface visibility is valuable.

**Reject**
- Generic “all your data in one place / clear insights / confident decisions” copy.
- Repetitive feature-grid storytelling.

#### OpenBB
Source: https://openbb.co/

**Take**
- One strong thesis can carry a technically complex financial product.
- Restraint and concrete interface evidence can coexist.

**Reject**
- Infrastructure, governance, or AI messaging unless those become verified TraderFrame capabilities.

### Opportunity gap
Most category leaders sell **breadth, speed, data, customization, execution, or integration**. TraderFrame has a stronger naming opportunity: make **framing** the product metaphor.

> Raw market movement exists everywhere. TraderFrame's visual language should make the act of selecting context, structure, and relevance feel tangible.

That is more ownable than another dark trading terminal with floating charts.

## 4. Design Read

**Business**  
TraderFrame is currently treated as a trading / market-analysis product concept. Exact commercial scope remains unresolved.

**Emotional target**  
Focused, alert, deliberate, controlled. Never casino-like, breathless, or macho.

**Category expectation**  
Dark data surfaces, dense charts, green/red price movement, speed claims, multi-panel dashboards, “pro trader” language.

**Opportunity**  
Turn the name into the interface idea: **the frame separates signal from surrounding noise**.

**Memorable idea**  
A market field exists beyond the interface. When it passes through the TraderFrame, structure resolves: lines sharpen, hierarchy appears, and Vermilion marks the event that matters.

**Risk**  
- Too cinematic → becomes another speculative fintech trailer.
- Too dark → loses information hierarchy.
- Too much red → feels like loss/alarm/gaming.
- Too much 3D → product credibility drops.
- Too little real UI → looks like a motion studio instead of software.

## 6. EXPLORE — four materially different directions

### 01 — After Hours
A dark, nocturnal trading environment: sparse desk-light energy, market glow, cinematic tension, TraderFrame UI emerging from darkness.

**Strength:** immediate mood and premium finish.  
**Weakness:** the category already owns this aesthetic. It says “serious trading product” but not “TraderFrame.”  
**Decision:** **REJECT**.

### 02 — The Frame
A market field moves behind a fixed spatial frame. Outside the frame, movement is lower-contrast and unresolved. Inside it, structure sharpens, key relationships become legible, and Vermilion marks the relevant change. The hero resolves into a flat concept UI.

**Strength:** directly tied to the name; motion has explanatory purpose; can become a repeatable brand behavior.  
**Weakness:** needs disciplined transition into product UI or it becomes an art film.  
**Decision:** **SELECT**.

### 03 — Decision Surface
Almost no spectacle. Paper/black/red editorial typography, severe whitespace, large product crops, market states treated like an information publication.

**Strength:** clearest product credibility and best restraint.  
**Weakness:** not ownable enough by itself; could suit many analytics products.  
**Decision:** **REJECT AS PRIMARY DIRECTION**. Preserve one rule only: after the hero, interface clarity wins.

### 04 — Pressure Field
Candlesticks, volume planes, depth layers, and red thresholds compress toward a breakout/decision point in 3D space.

**Strength:** visually forceful and motion-rich.  
**Weakness:** spectacle starts to lead the product; risks trading-game energy and familiar fintech 3D.  
**Decision:** **REJECT**.

## 7. Specialist Council

### Brand Strategist
02 is strongest because it turns the name into behavior. 01 and 04 could be renamed to almost any trading product without changing the concept.

### Art Director
Select 02. Use one high-quality spatial sequence, then remove spectacle. Vermilion must function as an event color, not a wash or glow.

### Product Designer
02 works only if the transition lands in a believable, legible interface quickly. Borrow the **restraint rule** from 03, not its visual identity.

### Copywriter
Avoid “trade smarter,” “edge,” “alpha,” “power,” “precision,” and “confident decisions.” The name can carry the core verb: **Frame the market.**

### Creative Skeptic
Kill any version of 02 that is merely a rectangle floating over candles. The frame must visibly change information state; otherwise the metaphor is decorative rationalization.

### Independent reviewers
No blocker in 02 if:
1. the frame creates an observable before/after information state;
2. concept UI is labeled illustrative until real product screens exist;
3. product claims remain conservative;
4. reduced-motion users receive the same hierarchy without the cinematic sequence.

### COUNCIL VERDICT
**Recommendation:** Select **02 — The Frame**.  
**Why:** highest name/product fit and strongest potential for a recognizable system behavior.  
**Strongest objection:** it can become design-theater if product UI is delayed or weak.  
**Kill criteria:** if the frame cannot explain a useful information-state change in under ~3 seconds, abandon the device and fall back to direct product UI.  
**Confidence:** 0.84.  
**Next action:** prototype the hero-to-interface transition and review it on desktop/mobile/reduced-motion.

## 8. Selected Creative Direction — THE FRAME

### Direction statement
**TraderFrame treats market structure as something you actively frame, not passively watch.** The marketing surface begins with unresolved movement, introduces a deliberate frame, and then resolves into a precise product surface. The experience moves from field → context → structure → decision.

### Non-negotiables
- The frame must change information state.
- Vermilion is reserved for meaningful change, focus, or action.
- Marketing 3D never leaks into core UI chrome.
- No green/red casino palette.
- No fake live data or unverified performance metrics.
- No generic glowing orb, AI network, floating glass card, city-at-night trader imagery, or metallic candlestick hero.
- No fade-up spam or permanent parallax.

### Typography
- Space Grotesk: large brand/action statements.
- Inter: explanatory/product text.
- IBM Plex Mono: labels, state, timestamps, market notation.
- Use scale and alignment, not many font weights, to create hierarchy.

## 9. Landing-page architecture

### 01 / Hero — FRAME THE MARKET
Headline: **Frame the market.**  
Support: **A focused trading workspace built around context, structure, and decision clarity.**  
Primary action: **View the workspace**  
Secondary: **See the system**

Hero motion: unresolved market field → frame enters/locks → information inside frame sharpens → one Vermilion event is promoted → scene resolves into interface.

### 02 / Thesis — NOISE → STRUCTURE → DECISION
Three large states, not three feature cards. Explain the product philosophy before listing capabilities that are not yet verified.

### 03 / Concept UI — THE WORKSPACE
A clearly labeled **CONCEPT UI / NOT LIVE DATA** surface demonstrates information hierarchy. It is evidence of the design direction, not a claim that those modules ship today.

### 04 / Interface principles
1. **Keep context on screen.**
2. **Promote change, not chrome.**
3. **Use motion to explain state.**

### 05 / Motion behavior
Show how framing, focus, and state transitions work. Reduced-motion equivalent is static framing + hierarchy change.

### 06 / Close
**Enter the frame.**  
Prototype note remains visible until product truth is validated.

## 10. Motion choreography

Hero state machine:

`AMBIENT FIELD → FRAME ACQUIRE → RESOLVE → SIGNAL EVENT → UI SETTLE → IDLE`

- **Ambient field:** slow market geometry; low contrast; no decorative looping particles.
- **Frame acquire:** frame moves only enough to demonstrate selection/context.
- **Resolve:** content inside frame becomes more legible; outside remains subordinate.
- **Signal event:** one Vermilion event, short and controlled.
- **UI settle:** camera/field collapses into a flat dashboard plane.
- **Idle:** motion nearly stops.

Reduced motion:
`STATIC FIELD → FRAME PRESENT → UI PRESENT`

No essential information depends on animation.
