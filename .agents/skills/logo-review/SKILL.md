# logo-review

## Purpose
Judge whether a logo concept is strategically relevant, visually ownable, technically reproducible, and strong enough to advance into a canonical identity system.

## When to use
- After concept-family exploration.
- Before finalist approval and canonical vector lock.
- After substantial geometry/wordmark refinement.
- Whenever a generated concept looks impressive but may be generic or structurally weak.

## Inputs required
- Brand strategy and desired perceptions.
- Seven-type assessment and selected concept family.
- Candidate symbol/wordmark in black/white and intended color.
- 16/32/64/128px views or equivalent reduction evidence.
- Inspiration/competitor references and originality screen.
- Reproduction/application requirements.

## Operating principles
- Review the mark before reading its symbolism explanation.
- Separate concept weakness from execution defects.
- A strong presentation board cannot compensate for weak geometry or a generic silhouette.
- Small-size, monochrome, one-color, reverse, and low-detail reproduction are mandatory evidence.
- Complexity is contextual; reject unnecessary detail, not complexity by ideology.
- Do not clear legal trademark risk—this is design/confusion screening only.
- Severity: BLOCKER / MAJOR / MINOR / TASTE.

## Workflow
1. Perform a 1-second silhouette/read test without rationale.
2. Compare the read to the intended brand perception and memory hook.
3. Evaluate concept originality and category confusion risk.
4. Inspect geometry, negative space, optical balance, curve/angle tension, counters, spacing, and wordmark relationship.
5. Test 16/32/64/128px, one-color, inverse, favicon, signage, print, and low-detail reproduction.
6. Check whether responsive variants preserve identity rather than becoming unrelated marks.
7. Review motion potential only if motion derives from structure.
8. Classify findings and return APPROVE / REVISE / REJECT.

## Deliverables
- Cold-read and first associations.
- Concept-strength finding.
- Distinctiveness/confusion finding.
- Optical/geometry findings.
- Typography/lockup findings.
- Small-size/reproduction findings.
- AI-generic risk assessment.
- Decision and prioritized corrections.

## Review criteria
- Memorable proprietary silhouette.
- Strategic relevance without forced symbolism.
- Strong monochrome performance.
- Small-size legibility.
- Optical quality.
- Wordmark compatibility.
- Reproduction resilience.
- Low confusion/copy and AI-generic risk.

## Failure modes
- Approving because the rationale sounds smart.
- Scoring only aesthetics at large presentation size.
- Ignoring wordmark spacing/typography.
- Treating every geometric imperfection as a blocker when it is an intentional optical correction.
- Demanding hidden-letter cleverness.
- Mistaking trend familiarity for brand fit.

## Handoffs
- `logo-designer` revises validated issues.
- `brand-fit-review` checks strategic coherence.
- `creative-skeptic` challenges high-risk finalist selection.
- `logo-integrity` begins only after explicit approval of canonical geometry.
